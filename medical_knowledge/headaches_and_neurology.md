# Clinical Reference Guide: Headaches and Cranial Discomfort

**Source**: International Headache Society (IHS) & National Institute of Neurological Disorders and Stroke (NINDS)  
**Reading Level Target**: Plain English with Clinical Grounding  
**Specialty Recommendation**: Neurologist / General Physician

---

## 1. Overview of Common Primary Headaches
Headaches are among the most prevalent neurological complaints. The vast majority are primary benign headaches, wherein the headache itself is the condition (rather than a symptom of an underlying structural disease). Evaluating the character, location, triggers, and duration is key.

### Common Associated Symptoms
- Aching, throbbing, or pressing sensations around forehead, temples, or back of head
- Neck stiffness or shoulder muscle tightness
- Mild light sensitivity (photophobia) or sound sensitivity (phonophobia)
- Scalp tenderness or facial pressure behind the eyes

### Possible Common Ailments Associated with Headaches
1. **Tension-Type Headache**:
   - Symptoms: Constant, dull, non-pulsatile ache felt like a tight band or vise squeezing around both sides of the head. Mild to moderate intensity; not aggravated by routine physical activity.
   - Typical Triggers: Prolonged screen time, mental stress, sleep deprivation, poor ergonomic posture.
   - Recommended Specialist: Neurologist / General Physician.

2. **Migraine without Aura**:
   - Symptoms: Moderate-to-severe throbbing or pulsating pain typically on one side of the head (unilateral), aggravated by walking or climbing stairs. Frequently accompanied by nausea, sensitivity to bright lights, and loud sounds.
   - Typical Course: Lasts between 4 to 72 hours if untreated.
   - Recommended Specialist: Neurologist.

3. **Sinus Headache (Rhinosinusitis-Associated)**:
   - Symptoms: Dull ache and heavy facial pressure centered around forehead, cheekbones, and behind nasal bridge. Pain intensifies when bending forward. Accompanied by nasal congestion or discolored nasal discharge.
   - Typical Course: Resolves as sinus inflammation clears.
   - Recommended Specialist: ENT Specialist or General Physician.

## Red Flag Symptoms (Seek Immediate Emergency Care - SNOOP Criteria)
Immediate emergency evaluation is imperative if:
- "Thunderclap headache": Sudden, excruciating headache reaching peak intensity within 60 seconds (described as the "worst headache of life")
- Headache accompanied by neurological deficits: unilateral weakness, speech slurring, vision loss, or facial droop (Stroke suspicion)
- Headache with high fever, neck rigidity, and confusion (Meningitis suspicion)
- New headache starting after age 50 or in a patient with a history of cancer
- Headache triggered by coughing, sneezing, or bearing down

## General Non-Prescription Supportive Care
- **Quiet, Dark Environment**: Resting in a dimly lit, quiet room significantly attenuates sensory overload during acute headaches.
- **Hydration & Electrolytes**: Drink water promptly; mild dehydration is a frequent headache trigger.
- **Cold or Warm Compresses**: Place a cold gel pack over the forehead/temples or a warm heating pad across tight neck muscles.
- **Digital Rest**: Take regular breaks from computer and smartphone screens (e.g. 20-20-20 rule).
