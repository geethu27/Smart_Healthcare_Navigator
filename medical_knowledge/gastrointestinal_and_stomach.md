# Clinical Reference Guide: Gastrointestinal Symptoms and Stomach Pain

**Source**: National Institute of Diabetes and Digestive and Kidney Diseases (NIDDK) & NHS Guidelines  
**Reading Level Target**: Plain English with Clinical Grounding  
**Specialty Recommendation**: Gastroenterologist / General Physician

---

## 1. Overview of Abdominal Discomfort & Stomach Pain
Abdominal discomfort can originate from hollow visceral organs (stomach, intestines, gallbladder) due to distension, mucosal inflammation, hyperacidity, or motility disorders. Identifying the location, timing (e.g., after food), and associated symptoms is essential.

### Common Associated Symptoms
- Upper or lower abdominal cramping, dull aching, or burning
- Heartburn (retrosternal burning feeling) and acid regurgitation
- Bloating, flatulence, and feeling overly full after small portions
- Nausea, loss of appetite, or mild vomiting
- Alternating bowel habits (diarrhea or constipation)

### Possible Common Ailments Associated with Abdominal Symptoms
1. **Gastroenteritis (Stomach Infection / Stomach Bug)**:
   - Symptoms: Rapid onset of watery diarrhea, stomach cramps, nausea, vomiting, mild fever, and generalized fatigue.
   - Typical Course: 24 to 72 hours, usually caused by Norovirus, Rotavirus, or mild food contamination.
   - Recommended Specialist: Gastroenterologist / General Physician.

2. **Gastroesophageal Reflux Disease (GERD) / Acid Reflux**:
   - Symptoms: Burning chest pain (heartburn) radiating upwards behind the breastbone, sour taste in mouth, worsening pain when lying flat or bending over, throat irritation.
   - Typical Triggers: Fatty foods, citrus, caffeine, eating immediately prior to sleep.
   - Recommended Specialist: Gastroenterologist.

3. **Gastritis / Dyspepsia (Indigestion)**:
   - Symptoms: Gnawing or burning ache in upper abdomen, early satiety, burping, mild nausea after spicy or acidic meals.
   - Typical Triggers: Stress, NSAID painkillers, irregular meal schedules.
   - Recommended Specialist: Gastroenterologist / General Physician.

## Red Flag Symptoms (Seek Immediate Emergency Care)
Immediate emergency consultation is required if any of the following occur:
- Sudden, severe, unbearable abdominal pain (rigid "board-like" abdomen)
- Hematemesis (vomiting blood or material resembling dark coffee grounds)
- Melena (tarry, jet-black, foul-smelling stools) or visible rectal bleeding
- Jaundice (yellow discoloration of whites of eyes and skin)
- Inability to pass stool or gas accompanied by severe distension and persistent vomiting

## General Non-Prescription Supportive Care
- **BRAT Diet**: When experiencing acute stomach upset, stick to Bland foods: Bananas, Rice, Applesauce, and Toast.
- **Electrolyte Replenishment**: Sip oral rehydration salts (ORS), coconut water, or diluted broths in small amounts every 15 minutes.
- **Meal Modifications for Acid Reflux**: Eat smaller, frequent meals; avoid eating within 3 hours of bedtime; elevate the head of the bed by 6 inches.
- **Avoid Irritants**: Refrain from alcohol, smoking, strong spices, and caffeinated beverages until symptoms resolve.
