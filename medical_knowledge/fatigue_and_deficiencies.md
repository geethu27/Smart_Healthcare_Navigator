# Clinical Reference Guide: Fatigue, Nutritional Deficiencies, and Blood Health

**Source**: National Institutes of Health (NIH) & World Health Organization (WHO) Nutritional Guidelines  
**Reading Level Target**: Plain English with Clinical Grounding  
**Specialty Recommendation**: Hematologist / General Physician / Clinical Dietitian

---

## 1. Overview of Chronic Fatigue & Blood Biomarkers
Fatigue is a feeling of constant tiredness, lack of energy, or physical exhaustion that does not resolve completely with adequate sleep. Common clinical contributors include micronutrient deficiencies (specifically Iron and Vitamin B12), thyroid fluctuations, and chronic physiological stress.

### Common Associated Symptoms
- Persistent exhaustion, sluggishness, and low physical stamina
- Pale or sallow skin (pallor), particularly noticeable on inner eyelids and nail beds
- Cold hands and feet
- Mild shortness of breath during routine mild activity (e.g. climbing one flight of stairs)
- Brittle nails, hair thinning, or mild dizziness when standing up quickly

### Possible Common Ailments Associated with Fatigue
1. **Iron Deficiency / Iron-Deficiency Anemia**:
   - Symptoms: Low serum ferritin and low hemoglobin resulting in reduced oxygen delivery to tissues. Manifests as lethargy, pale conjunctiva, brittle spoon-shaped nails (koilonychia), and cold intolerance.
   - Recommended Specialist: Hematologist / General Physician.
   - Dietary Guidance: Increase intake of iron-rich foods (spinach, lentils, chickpeas, lean poultry, pumpkin seeds, fortified cereals). Pair non-heme iron with Vitamin C (citrus, bell peppers, tomatoes) to enhance bioavailability.

2. **Vitamin B12 or Folate Deficiency**:
   - Symptoms: Fatigue, mild brain fog, mild tingling or numbness in extremities (peripheral neuropathy), sore red tongue (glossitis).
   - Recommended Specialist: General Physician / Neurologist.

3. **Physiological Stress / Sleep Architecture Deficit**:
   - Symptoms: Daytime drowsiness, morning exhaustion, brain fog, muscular tension, irritable mood without laboratory biomarker abnormalities.
   - Recommended Specialist: Family Medicine Physician.

## Red Flag Symptoms (Seek Immediate Emergency Care)
Seek immediate emergency medical attention if:
- Extreme dizziness or syncope (fainting / loss of consciousness)
- Irregular heart palpitations, rapid tachycardia at rest, or chest tightness
- Noticeable blood in stool, vomiting blood, or sudden severe unexplained weight loss
- Severe shortness of breath even when resting flat in bed

## Laboratory Panel Reference Ranges (Standard Adult CBC / Iron Panel)
- **Hemoglobin (Hb)**:
  - Normal Adult Male: 13.5 - 17.5 g/dL
  - Normal Adult Female: 12.0 - 15.5 g/dL
  - *Low indicates potential anemia / reduced oxygen transport.*
- **Serum Ferritin (Iron Storage)**:
  - Normal: 30 - 300 ng/mL
  - *Below 30 ng/mL strongly suggests depleted iron stores.*
- **Serum Iron**:
  - Normal: 60 - 170 mcg/dL
  - *Below 60 mcg/dL suggests active iron deficiency.*
- **White Blood Cell Count (WBC)**:
  - Normal: 4,500 - 11,000 cells/mcL
  - *Elevated indicates active immune or inflammatory response.*
- **Platelets**:
  - Normal: 150,000 - 450,000 /mcL
