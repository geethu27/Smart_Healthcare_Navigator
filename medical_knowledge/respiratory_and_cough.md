# Clinical Reference Guide: Respiratory Symptoms, Cough, and Sore Throat

**Source**: World Health Organization (WHO) & American Academy of Family Physicians (AAFP) Guidelines  
**Reading Level Target**: Plain English with Clinical Grounding  
**Specialty Recommendation**: Pulmonologist / Ear, Nose, Throat (ENT) Specialist / General Physician

---

## 1. Overview of Cough and Sore Throat
Coughing is a vital protective physiological reflex that clears irritants, foreign particles, and excessive secretions from the airways. A sore throat (pharyngitis) involves inflammation of the mucosal membranes lining the posterior pharynx, commonly precipitated by viral infections or environmental allergies.

### Common Associated Symptoms
- Throat irritation, pain on swallowing (odynophagia), or scratchiness
- Wet/productive cough (with clear or yellowish mucus) or dry persistent tickle
- Nasal congestion, rhinorrhea (runny nose), and post-nasal drip
- Sneezing, hoarseness, and mild low-grade fever
- Mild chest tightness from frequent coughing

### Possible Common Ailments Associated with Cough & Throat Pain
1. **Common Cold (Viral Rhinosinusitis)**:
   - Symptoms: Scratchy sore throat starting on day 1-2, followed by runny nose, nasal blockage, sneezing, and a mild dry cough.
   - Typical Course: 7 to 10 days. Self-limiting.
   - Recommended Specialist: General Physician.

2. **Acute Bronchitis (Chest Cold)**:
   - Symptoms: Persistent deep cough that often turns productive with sputum, mild chest soreness, fatigue, low-grade fever, and wheezing.
   - Typical Course: Cough often persists for 2 to 3 weeks following airway inflammation.
   - Recommended Specialist: Pulmonologist or General Physician.

3. **Allergic Rhinitis / Post-Nasal Drip**:
   - Symptoms: Throat tickling, frequent throat clearing, dry night-time cough, watery itchy eyes, clear nasal discharge, absence of fever.
   - Typical Course: Intermittent or seasonal depending on allergen exposure (pollen, dust mites, pet dander).
   - Recommended Specialist: Allergist / ENT Specialist.

## Red Flag Symptoms (Seek Immediate Emergency Care)
Seek immediate emergency medical attention if the patient experiences:
- Stridor, high-pitched whistling during inhalation, or severe shortness of breath
- Hemoptysis (coughing up bright red blood or dark clots)
- Cyanosis (bluish tint to lips, nails, or face indicating low oxygen saturation)
- Inability to swallow saliva or open the mouth (trismus), which may indicate a peritonsillar abscess
- Severe persistent chest pain or dizziness

## General Non-Prescription Supportive Care
- **Steam Inhalation & Humidification**: Use a cool-mist humidifier or inhale steam from a warm shower to lubricate vocal cords and loosen phlegm.
- **Warm Saltwater Gargle**: Dissolve 1/2 teaspoon of salt in 8 ounces of warm water and gargle 3 times daily to soothe inflamed pharyngeal tissue.
- **Honey & Warm Teas**: A spoonful of natural honey (in individuals aged 1 year and older) can coat and suppress an irritated cough reflex.
- **Hydration**: Maintain constant intake of warm fluids to thin mucus.
