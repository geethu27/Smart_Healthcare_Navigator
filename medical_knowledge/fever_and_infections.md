# Clinical Reference Guide: Fever and Common Acute Viral Infections

**Source**: Centers for Disease Control and Prevention (CDC) & National Health Service (NHS) Clinical Pathways  
**Reading Level Target**: Plain English with Clinical Grounding  
**Specialty Recommendation**: General Physician / Internal Medicine

---

## 1. Overview of Fever (Pyrexia)
A fever is a temporary elevation of normal body temperature (typically defined as an oral temperature of 100.4°F / 38°C or higher). It represents an active physiological immune response designed to combat viral or bacterial pathogens by stimulating leukocytes and inhibiting microbial replication.

### Common Associated Symptoms
- Body chills, shivering, and cold sensations
- Generalized sweating and warm skin
- Mild to moderate headache
- Generalized muscle aches (myalgia) and joint discomfort
- Fatigue, lethargy, and mild loss of appetite
- Mild dehydration signs (dry mouth, increased thirst)

### Possible Common Ailments Associated with Fever
1. **Acute Viral Upper Respiratory Infection (Viral Fever / Common Cold)**:
   - Symptoms: Low-to-moderate fever, nasal congestion, runny nose, sneezing, scratchy throat, mild dry cough.
   - Typical Course: Usually resolves spontaneously within 5 to 7 days with rest and hydration.
   - Recommended Specialist: General Physician / Family Medicine.

2. **Influenza (The Flu)**:
   - Symptoms: Abrupt onset of higher fever (101°F-104°F), intense muscle aches, severe fatigue, dry cough, chills, and headache.
   - Typical Course: Acute symptoms last 3 to 7 days; fatigue may linger for up to 2 weeks.
   - Recommended Specialist: General Physician / Pulmonologist if breathing difficulty occurs.

3. **Gastroenteritis (Stomach Flu)**:
   - Symptoms: Low-grade fever accompanied by nausea, loose watery stools, abdominal cramping, and occasional vomiting.
   - Recommended Specialist: General Physician or Gastroenterologist.

## Red Flag Symptoms (Seek Immediate Emergency Care)
Patients must be immediately directed to emergency care (dial 911/112/local emergency) if any of the following are present:
- High fever exceeding 103°F (39.4°C) that does not decrease with fluids or rest
- Stiff neck, extreme photophobia (pain when looking at light), and altered mental state or confusion
- Difficulty breathing, shortness of breath, or chest pressure
- Persistent vomiting with inability to keep any fluids down for over 24 hours
- Development of a new purpuric or dark purple rash that does not fade under pressure

---

## General Non-Prescription Supportive Care
- **Hydration**: Drink plentiful clear fluids (water, oral rehydration solutions, clear broths) to replace fluid lost through perspiration.
- **Adequate Rest**: Ensure ample sleep and avoid strenuous physical exertion.
- **Cooling Measures**: Wear lightweight, breathable clothing; keep room temperature comfortable; do not take ice baths.
- **Medical Follow-Up**: If fever persists for more than 3 consecutive days, a licensed doctor must evaluate the underlying cause.
